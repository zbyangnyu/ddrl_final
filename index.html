Embodied Reasoning Pretraining Enhances Vision-Language-Action Models

Beichen Wang, Zhenbang Yang, Jiayi Feng
NYU Courant
DDMRL 2025 Spring
Paper Supplementary Code arXiv

Abstract

Most state-of-the-art systems like OpenVLA, Octo, and pi0 fine-tune vanilla VLMs, such as LLaVA or PaLI-Gemma, using paired data of a single 2D image and a 3D end-effector position. However, these models often underperform in practice—especially in low-data settings. We believe one reason is that VLMs are fundamentally designed for pixel-level reasoning, not for grounded 3D control. A single 2D image simply lacks the spatial richness to support reliable action generation in 3D space. To address this, we introduce a bridge: before training the model to output 3D actions, we first teach it to reason about 2D trajectories, a step we call embodied reasoning.

2D Trajectory Training Dataset

Our training data comes from the DROID dataset. We project the 3D end-effector positions into image space using camera intrinsics and extrinsics. Since the raw trajectories are often noisy due to robot jitter, we apply downsampling to make them more learnable. This pretraining step aligns well with the VLM’s original 2D-focused architecture and gives it a lightweight yet grounded way to develop spatial reasoning before full 3D fine-tuning.

Experiments and Evaluation

For evaluation, we use the Simpler benchmark based on ManiSkill2. Due to compute limitations, we focused only on pick-and-place tasks with minimal data. After pretraining PaLI-Gemma, we loaded the weights into pi0’s training pipeline. Although our model did not match pi0’s official performance—primarily due to our limited resources—it still outperformed a vanilla baseline without 2D trajectory pretraining. This suggests that even lightweight embodied pretraining can provide meaningful improvements when scaling is limited.

Conclusion

In summary, we argue that directly mapping 2D images to 3D actions may be fundamentally limited, given the pixel-based nature of current VLMs. By introducing a lightweight embodied reasoning stage—through 2D trajectory pretraining—we provide a more structured path from perception to action. While our current results are constrained by data and compute, the early gains are promising. We hope this work inspires future research into more scalable, geometry-aware pretraining strategies for general-purpose robot learning.
